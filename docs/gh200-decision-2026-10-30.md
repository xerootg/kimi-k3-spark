K3 padding is computable. All-IQ1_S records are 6451200 B with no padding; mixed IQ2_XXS gate/up records are 7827456 B in 10137600 B slots, wasting 22.79% of allocated space; IQ2_XXS-down records are 7139328 B in the same slots, wasting 29.58%. Across 80/10/2 layers, the existing proportional allocation yields the +3.220% slots above; byte strides equal record sizes. These are RAM-slot savings, not a disk-store repack (docs/gh200-glm-plan.md, capacity arithmetic).

That calculation assumes the nominal 424000 MiB host ceiling; the separate 24 GiB HBM tier and valve releases change effective residency. Extend the audited IQ1_S/IQ2_XXS allowlist and validate CPU-slot addressing, or measure the GPU-map tradeoff explicitly. Use matched frozen snapshots, idle greedy runs with cache_prompt:false and gold ac78af0e5d, plus concurrent prefill, restore and pressure gates (docs/gh200-glm-plan.md; docs/experiments.md).

Do not count existing wins again: Q6_K TMA already measured 12.99 → 13.25 tok/s (+2%), bit-identical. Prefill lookahead already reaches 127–145 tok/s at the 9.6 GB/s read ceiling. Ubatch 4096 reached 185.9 tok/s but left only 1.8 GB HBM and was rejected; shrinking the HBM tier trades decode hits for prefill room and requires both measurements (docs/gh200-glm-plan.md; docs/experiments.md, TMA and prefill-past-ceiling entries).

For GLM, the owner's rounded 96 GB HBM + 441 GB host totals 537 GB, already below 562.46 GB before runtime allocations (arithmetic from docs/gh200-glm-plan.md). The ledger actually specifies 431 GiB host; keep units explicit. The plan budgets 28.111 GB spine in HBM, 400.312 GB host experts (~74.92% of the active store), and 85.666 GB total HBM including estimated KV/workspace/reserves. Reclaiming the host spine copy might raise expert residency to 80.18%; neither capacity scenario is a measured hit rate (docs/experiments.md; docs/gh200-glm-plan.md).

The requested 12–15 tok/s GLM estimate is absent from this checkout's roadmap/status; the status instead cites the plan's 4–9 tok/s with ~2 downside. Treat 12–15 as an unverified target, not evidence. K3's measured speed does not establish GLM throughput, and its dense Q6_K TMA kernel does not automatically accelerate routed GLM experts (docs/roadmap.md; docs/GLM-STATUS.md; docs/gh200-glm-plan.md).

Prepare and SHA-check NAS shards, build the store with tools/repack-experts.py and verify it, then create a GLM-specific warm snapshot; retain K3 artifacts, configuration and snapshot for rollback. GGUF plus active store needs ~1.097 TB. The plan estimates ~15–30 min for repack/verification from the GH200 K3 precedent; Spark's ~2 min GLM Q2 repack is not a GH200 Q5 timing (docs/gh200-glm-plan.md; docs/experiments.md).

No measured NAS-to-GH200 WAN rate is documented. The plan's K3 download precedent implies ~165 MB/s and ~57 min for GLM, budgeting ~1–2 h with checks/retries; it is not a NAS measurement. NAS-to-Spark measured ~60 MB/s would imply ~2.6 h for 562.46 GB if sustained, but that local NFS rate does not predict the WAN. Stage artifacts before the serving outage where resources permit; K3 remains down throughout the actual GLM benchmark (docs/gh200-glm-plan.md; docs/experiments.md, GLM weights entry).

Recommendation: keep K3 production, prepare the bounded byte-stride test first, and take its reversible window only after the addressing audit and rollback are ready. Defer the larger GLM window until transfer, capacity and outage budgets are concrete; neither option warrants immediate production replacement (docs/gh200-glm-plan.md).
